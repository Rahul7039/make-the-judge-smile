# Candidates — Make the Judge Smile

Judge brief, in order: (1) subject — one line, ≤200 chars, about a small
good thing that happened today; (2) warmth — a glow, a kindness or piece of
luck that feels genuinely felt; (3) surprise — a turn you did not expect, in
the thing or in the telling; (4) concreteness — one real small detail, a
name, a sound, a time of day; (5) fresh wording — its own words and rhythm,
no greeting-card phrasing.

## 1. The chaiwala (142 chars)

> At 7 a.m. the chaiwala remembered my order, poured an extra half cup, and refused the extra ten rupees — said today the first smile is on him.

- Warmth: strong — remembered order, free extra cup, refused payment.
- Surprise: good — the refusal of money flips the expected transaction.
- Concreteness: strong — 7 a.m., extra half cup, ten rupees.
- Fresh wording: decent, but "the first smile is on him" leans slightly cute.
- Weakness: two kindnesses stacked (extra cup + refused money) dilutes the single turn.

## 2. The neighbour's cat (140 chars)

> The neighbour's cat, who has ignored me for a year, hopped onto my lap this morning and fell asleep mid-purr like we'd been friends forever.

- Warmth: strong — an animal choosing you is a pure glow.
- Surprise: strong — a year of being ignored, reversed in one hop.
- Concreteness: strong — a year, mid-purr, this morning.
- Fresh wording: good — "like we'd been friends forever" is its own rhythm.
- Weakness: no human kindness; warmth is affection, not generosity, and the judge's brief leans toward kindness.

## 3. The milkman (125 chars)

> The power cut killed my alarm, but the milkman knocked twice at 5:30 and woke me in time for the one meeting I couldn't miss.

- Warmth: moderate — a rescue, but luck more than kindness.
- Surprise: strong — the setup (power cut) becomes the save; the knock is the turn.
- Concreteness: strong — knocked twice, 5:30, one meeting.
- Fresh wording: good, plain and unadorned.
- Weakness: the milkman did nothing extraordinary — he just knocked. The glow is thin.

## 4. The stranger at the signal (126 chars)

> A stranger at the signal held up traffic with one hand so an old man could cross, then bowed to him like he'd just met a king.

- Warmth: strong — a stranger spending his own safety for an old man.
- Surprise: good — the bow at the end reframes the act as reverence, not duty.
- Concreteness: strong — one hand, the signal, the bow.
- Fresh wording: good — "like he'd just met a king" is fresh.
- Weakness: reads slightly staged; the bow risks feeling written rather than witnessed.

## 5. The ten-rupee note (143 chars)

> I found the ten-rupee note I lost last Diwali folded inside my winter jacket — same pocket, same crease, like it had been waiting to say hello.

- Warmth: weak — luck, not kindness; nobody gave anything.
- Surprise: good — lost last Diwali, found now, in the same pocket.
- Concreteness: strong — ten rupees, last Diwali, same crease.
- Fresh wording: very good — "like it had been waiting to say hello" is original.
- Weakness: fails warmth, the second criterion; a found note is a smile, not a glow.

## 6. The chalked sun (141 chars)

> The lift was broken, so I took the stairs and found my neighbour's kid had chalked a lopsided sun on every landing, just for whoever climbed.

- Warmth: strong — a child's anonymous gift to strangers.
- Surprise: good — a broken lift becomes a gallery.
- Concreteness: strong — every landing, lopsided sun, chalk.
- Fresh wording: good — "just for whoever climbed" is fresh.
- Weakness: "just for whoever climbed" is a guess about the kid's intent; slightly sentimental.

## 7. The auto driver — WINNER (137 chars)

> Today the auto driver turned the meter off at the last signal and said the ride was free — his daughter had passed her exam this morning.

- Warmth: strongest — a free ride given out of joy, not pity; his good news becomes my good luck.
- Surprise: strongest — the meter going off is the turn, and the reason (his daughter passed) is a second turn underneath it.
- Concreteness: strong — the last signal, the meter, this morning, the exam.
- Fresh wording: plain and unadorned, no greeting-card phrasing; the line trusts the facts to do the work.
- Why it wins: it is the only candidate where warmth and surprise feed each other — the kindness is caused by happiness, so the glow doubles. It scores highest on criteria 2 and 3, the two that decide most pairwise games after subject, while matching the others on detail and wording.
